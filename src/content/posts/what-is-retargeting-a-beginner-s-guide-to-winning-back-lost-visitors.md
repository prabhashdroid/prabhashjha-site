> Most people who visit your site leave without buying — that's normal. Retargeting is how you gently bring the interested ones back instead of losing them forever. Here's what it is, in plain language, and how to use it without being creepy.

## What retargeting actually is

Retargeting (also called remarketing) shows ads to people who already visited you but didn't act. They browsed, maybe added to cart, then left. A small tag on your site remembers them, and later they see a reminder of you while scrolling elsewhere. You're not chasing strangers — you're following up with people who already showed interest.

## Why it works so well

- **Warm, not cold.** These people already know you, so they convert far better than first-time strangers.

- **Cheap attention.** Reminding an interested visitor costs less than winning a brand-new one.

- **Timing.** Most people don't buy on the first visit; retargeting catches them when they're finally ready.

- **Recovers near-misses.** Abandoned carts and half-finished signups are the easiest wins you have.

## How to use it without being annoying

1. **Cap how often people see it.** The same ad ten times a day turns interest into irritation. Limit the frequency.

1. **Set a sensible window.** Someone who visited three months ago probably isn't shopping anymore. Focus on recent visitors.

1. **Match the message to where they left off.** A cart-abandoner needs a nudge; a blog reader needs a reason to come closer.

1. **Exclude people who already bought.** Nothing wastes money like advertising something to someone who just purchased it.

1. **Give a real reason to return** — an answer to their hesitation, not just your logo again.

## FAQs

### Is retargeting worth it for a small business?

Often yes — it's one of the highest-return uses of a small budget, because you're spending on people who already know you rather than paying to reach cold strangers.

### Does retargeting feel intrusive to customers?

It can if you overdo it. Cap the frequency, keep the window short, and stop showing ads once someone buys. Done with restraint, it feels like a helpful reminder, not stalking.

## Key takeaways

- Retargeting follows up with people who already showed interest.

- Warm visitors convert far better than cold strangers.

- Cap frequency, keep the window recent, and exclude buyers.

- Give a real reason to come back, not just another logo.
