> You can do everything right in affiliate marketing and still fail at one boring thing: tracking. If you don't know which link, post or channel actually earns, you're flying blind — and you'll pour effort into things that don't pay.

## How affiliate tracking works

When you join a program, you get a unique link with your ID baked in. When someone clicks it and buys within the cookie window, the sale is credited to you. Everything you do to “track” is really about knowing **which of your links and channels** produced those sales — so you can do more of what works.

## The simple way to track as a beginner

1. **Use the program's own dashboard first.** Every affiliate program shows clicks, conversions and earnings. Check it before buying any tools.

1. **Create separate links per channel.** A different link for your YouTube, your newsletter and your blog tells you which one converts.

1. **Add sub-IDs or tags where allowed.** Many programs let you label a link (e.g. “review-post”) so you can see exactly which content earned.

1. **Keep a simple sheet.** List what you published, the link used, and the result. Low-tech, but it reveals your winners fast.

1. **Watch the cookie window.** If sales aren't tracking, a short cookie window or a broken link is often why.

## What to do with the data

Tracking is only useful if it changes what you do. Once a month, look at what earned and what didn't. Double down on the channels and content types that convert, and quietly drop the ones that never do. That single habit separates affiliates who grow from ones who plateau.

## FAQs

### Do I need paid tracking software?

Not at the start. Program dashboards plus separate links and a simple sheet are enough until you're earning consistently. Add tools when the manual method genuinely can't keep up.

### Why aren't my sales showing up?

Usually a broken or wrong link, an expired cookie window, or the buyer clicking a competitor's link last. Test your own links regularly and use the freshest ones.

## Key takeaways

- Your affiliate link carries your ID — tracking is knowing which links earn.

- Start with the program dashboard, separate links, and sub-IDs.

- A simple sheet beats no tracking every time.

- Act on the data: do more of what converts, drop what doesn't.
