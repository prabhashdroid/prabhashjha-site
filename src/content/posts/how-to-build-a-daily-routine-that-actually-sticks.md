> Most people don't fail at routines because they're lazy — they fail because they build routines that were never realistic. A routine that sticks is small, specific and forgiving. Here's how to build one that actually lasts.

## Why most routines collapse

The usual mistake is starting too big: a five-hour morning overhaul on day one. It feels great for three days, then life happens and the whole thing breaks. Routines don't fail from lack of willpower; they fail from bad design. The fix is to start almost embarrassingly small and let it grow.

## How to build one that lasts

1. **Start tiny.** One or two small habits, not a total life redesign. Small is what survives a bad week.

1. **Anchor new habits to existing ones.** “After I make coffee, I write for ten minutes” beats a vague intention.

1. **Make it obvious and easy.** Lower the friction — lay things out, remove the excuses in advance.

1. **Protect the same time slot.** Consistency of when matters more than length.

1. **Allow off days without quitting.** Missing once is normal; missing twice is where routines die. Just restart.

## What actually makes it stick

Consistency beats intensity, every time. A ten-minute habit done daily for a year reshapes your life far more than a heroic burst that burns out in a week. Aim to be the person who shows up small and often, not the one who goes all-in and vanishes.

## FAQs

### How long does it take to build a habit?

Longer than the myth of “21 days” for most things, and it varies by person and habit. Don't count days — just focus on not missing twice in a row, and let it become automatic over time.

### What if I keep breaking my routine?

Your routine is probably too big or too vague. Shrink it until it's almost too easy to skip, anchor it to something you already do, and rebuild from there.

## Key takeaways

- Routines fail from bad design, not weak willpower.

- Start tiny and anchor new habits to existing ones.

- Protect the same time slot; consistency beats length.

- Missing once is fine — never miss twice in a row.
