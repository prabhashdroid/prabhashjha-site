> A profit and loss statement sounds like something only accountants read. It isn't. It's just a plain summary of what came in, what went out, and what was left — and once you can read it, you can actually run a business instead of guessing.

## What a P&L is

A profit and loss statement (also called an income statement) shows your business's money over a period — a month, a quarter, a year. Top line: what you earned. Then what it cost. Bottom line: what's left. That's it. The jargon hides a very simple story.

## The lines that matter

- **Revenue (top line)** — all the money your business brought in from sales.

- **Cost of goods sold (COGS)** — the direct cost of delivering what you sold.

- **Gross profit** — revenue minus COGS. What's left to run the business.

- **Operating expenses** — rent, salaries, software, marketing — the cost of keeping the lights on.

- **Net profit (bottom line)** — what remains after everything. The number that actually matters.

## How to read one like an owner

1. **Start at the bottom, then explain it.** Is there profit? Then work up to see why.

1. **Watch the margins, not just the totals.** Gross margin and net margin tell you how healthy each sale is.

1. **Compare over time.** One month means little; the trend across months tells the real story.

1. **Find the biggest costs.** A few line items usually drive most of your spending — that's where to look first.

## P&L vs cash flow

A P&L can show a profit while your bank account is empty, because it records sales you've made but not yet been paid for. That's why you read the P&L for profitability and watch cash flow for survival — they answer different questions.

## FAQs

### How often should I look at my P&L?

Monthly is a good rhythm for most small businesses. Often enough to catch problems early, not so often that normal ups and downs spook you.

### Do I need an accountant to understand it?

An accountant helps you produce it accurately, but reading it is a skill worth having yourself. Nobody understands your numbers' story better than you should.

## Key takeaways

- A P&L is a plain summary: money in, costs out, what's left.

- Know the key lines — revenue, gross profit, operating costs, net profit.

- Watch margins and trends, not just totals.

- Read the P&L for profit; watch cash flow for survival.
