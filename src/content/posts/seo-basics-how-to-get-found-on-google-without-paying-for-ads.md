> SEO — search engine optimisation — is how you get found on Google without paying for every click. It's slower than ads, but it compounds: one good article can bring visitors for years. Here are the basics, in plain language.

## What SEO actually is

SEO is making your pages easy for Google to understand and worth showing to people who are searching. Three things matter most: useful content, the right keywords, and other sites trusting you (links). Get those roughly right and you'll rank for terms that quietly send you free traffic.

## The basics that move the needle

1. **Target one clear keyword per page.** Write for a specific thing people search, like “how to start affiliate marketing”, not a vague topic.

1. **Answer the question fully.** Google rewards pages that genuinely help — cover the topic better than what's already ranking.

1. **Use a clear structure.** A descriptive title, headings, and short paragraphs. Easy to read is easy to rank.

1. **Get on-page basics right.** Put your keyword in the title, the first paragraph, and a heading — naturally, not stuffed.

1. **Earn links and mentions.** When useful content gets shared and linked, Google trusts you more. Being helpful is the long game.

1. **Be patient.** SEO takes months, then compounds. Publish consistently and it snowballs.

## SEO vs paid ads

Ads are fast but stop the moment you stop paying. SEO is slow but keeps working. The smart move is both — ads for quick results, SEO for the compounding asset. If you have more time than money, SEO wins over the long run.

## FAQs

### How long does SEO take to work?

Usually a few months to see traction, longer for competitive terms. It's a compounding asset, not a quick win — which is exactly why it's worth starting early.

### Do I need to be technical to do SEO?

No. The biggest wins are content and clarity: pick a keyword, answer it fully, structure it well. The technical parts matter far less than being genuinely useful.

## Key takeaways

- SEO gets you found on Google without paying per click.

- Target one keyword per page and answer it better than anyone.

- Clear titles, headings and structure do most of the work.

- It's slow, then it compounds — start early and stay consistent.
