> CPC, CPM, CTR, CPA, ROAS — marketing is full of initials that make simple ideas sound complicated. Here's what each one actually means, in plain English, so you can read any ad report without feeling lost.

## The metrics, decoded

- **CPM (cost per mille)** — what you pay per 1,000 times your ad is shown. A reach/awareness cost.

- **CPC (cost per click)** — what you pay each time someone clicks. Measures interest, not sales.

- **CTR (click-through rate)** — the share of people who clicked after seeing the ad. A quick read on whether your ad is compelling.

- **CPA (cost per acquisition)** — what you pay for one actual result: a lead or a sale. This is the one that matters.

- **ROAS (return on ad spend)** — how much revenue each rupee of ad spend brought back. 3x means ₹3 back for every ₹1 spent.

## Which ones actually matter

Beginners obsess over CPC and CTR because they move fast and feel exciting. But cheap clicks that never buy are worthless. **CPA and ROAS** are the metrics that tell you whether you're making or losing money. Judge campaigns by cost per result and return — not by vanity clicks.

## How they connect

They're a chain: your ad is shown (CPM), some people click (CTR → CPC), some of those buy (CPA), and the money they spend versus what you paid is your return (ROAS). A weak link anywhere breaks the chain — a great CTR with a bad landing page still gives you a terrible CPA.

## FAQs

### What's a “good” ROAS?

It depends on your margins. A business with fat margins can thrive at 2x; a thin-margin one might need 4x just to break even. Know your numbers before you copy someone else's benchmark.

### Should I optimise for clicks or conversions?

Conversions, almost always. Clicks are a means, not the goal. Optimise for the cheapest real result — a lead or a sale — not the cheapest click.

## Key takeaways

- CPM = cost to be seen; CPC = cost per click; CTR = how compelling the ad is.

- CPA and ROAS are the metrics that decide if you're profitable.

- Cheap clicks that don't convert are worthless.

- Know your margins before judging any benchmark.
