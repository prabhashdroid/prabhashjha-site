> Google Ads and Meta Ads (Facebook and Instagram) are the two big places beginners run paid ads — and they work in opposite ways. Which to start with comes down to one question: are people already searching for what you sell?

## The core difference

**Google Ads catches existing demand.** People type what they want, and you show up — high intent, they're already looking. **Meta Ads creates demand.** You interrupt people scrolling who weren't looking, and the creative has to make them care. One captures intent; the other manufactures it.

## When to start with Google Ads

- People already search for your product or solution.

- You sell something with clear, high buying intent — a service or a specific product.

- You want faster, more predictable results and can win on the offer.

## When to start with Meta Ads

- Your product is discovery- or impulse-led — people don't know to search for it.

- You have strong creative: video, images, a good hook.

- You want cheap reach to test angles and build demand.

## The honest answer

If people are already searching for what you offer, start with **Google** — capturing existing intent is the easiest money. If they're not, or your product is visual and impulse-driven, start with **Meta** and win on the creative. Either way: know your numbers first, start small, and only scale what's already profitable.

## FAQs

### Which is cheaper, Google or Meta?

It depends on your niche and competition. Google clicks can cost more but convert better (higher intent); Meta reach is cheaper but colder. Judge by cost per conversion, not cost per click.

### Can I run both?

Yes, and many businesses do — but not on day one. Win one channel first, prove the economics, then add the other.

## Key takeaways

- Google captures existing demand; Meta creates new demand.

- Start with Google if people already search for what you sell.

- Start with Meta if it's discovery-led and your creative is strong.

- Know your numbers, start small, scale only what's profitable.
