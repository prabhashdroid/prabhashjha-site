> Your bio is the most-read thing you'll ever write and the one most people write badly. It's on your site, your profiles, every intro. A good one earns trust in seconds. Here's how to write one that gets you noticed.

## Why your bio matters more than you think

People read your bio at the exact moment they're deciding whether you're worth their attention — before a call, a follow, a hire. A vague, buzzword-stuffed bio wastes that moment. A clear one tells them who you help, how, and why to trust you, fast.

## What a good bio includes

- **Who you are and what you do** — in plain words, not a job-title word salad.

- **Who you help and how** — the value you create for someone reading it.

- **A proof point** — something real that earns trust (experience, work, a track record) without bragging.

- **A bit of personality** — one human detail so you're a person, not a template.

- **A clear next step** — where to go or what to do next.

## How to write it

1. **Start with who you help, not your title.** “I help X do Y” beats “results-driven professional.”

1. **Cut the buzzwords.** “Passionate, dynamic, synergistic” says nothing. Be specific instead.

1. **Write it in your real voice** — the way you'd actually introduce yourself to someone.

1. **Keep versions.** A short one-liner, a medium paragraph, and a longer story for your About page.

1. **End with a next step** so a reader who's interested knows what to do.

## FAQs

### Should I write my bio in first or third person?

First person for your own site and social profiles — it feels personal. Third person for formal contexts like event intros or press. Keep both versions ready.

### How long should a bio be?

Have three lengths: a one-line version, a short paragraph, and a longer story. Different places need different lengths — the one-liner does most of the heavy lifting.

## Key takeaways

- Your bio is read at the moment people decide if you're worth it.

- Lead with who you help, not your job title.

- Cut buzzwords, add one real proof point and a human detail.

- Keep short, medium and long versions ready to use.
