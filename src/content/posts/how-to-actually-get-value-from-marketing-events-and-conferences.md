> Most people go to marketing events, collect a bag of swag and a stack of business cards, and get nothing from it. A few walk out with clients, partners and ideas that pay for the ticket a hundred times over. The difference isn't luck — it's a simple approach. Here's how I get real value from marketing events and conferences.

## Why most people waste marketing events

They show up with no goal, sit through talks passively, network randomly, and never follow up. The event itself isn't the value — it's the doorway. The value is in the specific people you meet, and what you do in the two weeks after.

## Before the event

- **Set one clear goal.** Three useful conversations, one partner, or one specific thing to learn. One goal beats ten vague ones.

- **Do your homework.** Check the speaker and attendee list; pick 5–10 people you genuinely want to meet, and why.

- **Prepare your one-liner.** A clear, human answer to “what do you do?” that invites a follow-up question.

## During the event

- **Prioritise hallways over stages.** The best conversations happen between sessions, not during them. Talks you can usually watch later.

- **Give before you ask.** Make an introduction, share a useful idea, solve a small problem. Generosity is the fastest way to be remembered.

- **Take notes on people, not just talks.** One line about each person and a reason to follow up.

- **Quality over quantity.** Five real conversations beat fifty forgettable ones.

## After the event — where the value actually is

1. **Follow up within 48 hours**, while you're still fresh in their memory. A short, specific, personal message — not a copy-paste.

1. **Reference the actual conversation** so they remember you, and suggest one clear next step.

1. **Act on what you learned.** Turn the best idea into one small experiment this week, before the motivation fades.

## FAQs

### Are marketing conferences worth it?

Yes — if you go with a goal and follow up. The ticket pays for itself through one good relationship or idea. Without a plan, it's an expensive day out.

### How do I network at events if I'm introverted?

You don't need to work the room. Have a few genuine one-on-one conversations, lead with curiosity and helpfulness, and follow up in writing — where introverts often shine. Depth beats volume.

## Key takeaways

- The event is the doorway; the value is the people and the follow-up.

- Set one clear goal and research who you want to meet.

- Prioritise hallway conversations; give before you ask.

- Follow up within 48 hours with something specific.

- Act on one idea fast, before the motivation fades.
