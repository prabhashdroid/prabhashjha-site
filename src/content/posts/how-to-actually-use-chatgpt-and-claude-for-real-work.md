> Most people use AI chatbots like a novelty — ask a question, get an answer, move on. The people getting real leverage use them like a junior teammate: to draft, summarise, brainstorm and automate the boring parts. Here's how to actually use ChatGPT and Claude for real work.

## The mindset shift: direct it, don't just chat with it

The biggest unlock isn't a magic prompt — it's treating AI as something you **direct**. Give it context, tell it the goal, show an example of what good looks like, and review its output like you would a junior's. Vague question in, vague answer out. Clear brief in, useful work out.

## What it's genuinely good at

- **Drafting** — first drafts of emails, posts, outlines, code. A rough draft in seconds that you then improve.

- **Summarising** — turning long documents, notes or threads into the key points.

- **Brainstorming** — angles, ideas, names, counter-arguments when you're stuck.

- **Explaining** — breaking down a hard topic in plain language, at your level.

- **Repetitive text work** — reformatting, rewriting, tidying, translating.

## How to get better output

1. **Give context.** Who it's for, what the goal is, what tone you want. The more it knows, the better it does.

1. **Show an example.** One sample of what “good” looks like beats a paragraph of instructions.

1. **Ask for a draft, then iterate.** Don't expect a perfect first try — refine it with follow-ups.

1. **Always review.** AI is confident even when it's wrong. You're the editor; it's the assistant.

1. **Reuse what works.** Save your best prompts and instructions so you're not starting from scratch each time.

## What to be careful about

AI can be confidently wrong, so check facts and never paste in sensitive data you wouldn't want stored. Use it to do the first 80% fast, then bring your judgment for the last 20% — that last 20% is where the value, and your edge, lives.

## FAQs

### Is ChatGPT or Claude better?

Both are excellent and improving fast. Rather than pick a “best”, pick the one you'll actually build a habit with — the leverage comes from using it well and often, not from the brand.

### Will using AI make my work generic?

Only if you let it write the final version. Use it for the draft and the grunt work, then add your voice, judgment and real experience. That's what keeps it yours.

## Key takeaways

- Treat AI as a junior teammate you direct, not a novelty you chat with.

- It's great at drafting, summarising, brainstorming and repetitive text work.

- Give context, show an example, iterate, and always review.

- Do the fast 80% with AI; bring your judgment for the last 20%.
