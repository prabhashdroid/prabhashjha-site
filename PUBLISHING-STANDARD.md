# How I research and publish your posts

The keyword engine is the **safety net** — it guarantees a floor if a post ever goes up without me. This document is the **actual standard**: what I do before writing a word.

---

## What I checked first, and what it told us

**Your Google Search Console has no query data.** Not "thin" — empty. 7 impressions in 7 days means Google has nothing to report. So there is no first-party data to mine yet; every keyword decision has to come from live search research until the site starts earning impressions.

Then I researched three candidate topics for real:

| Topic tested | What the results showed | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Break-even ROAS / contribution margin | Page 1 is entirely funded SaaS calculators — Triple Whale, Saras, Daymark, Eightx | **Don't compete** |
| "Good ROAS but not profitable" | Same companies, plus agency blogs, all covering it well | **Don't compete** |
| Affiliate marketing India, beginner | Flooded with content farms recycling the same list of programs | **Don't compete** |

But the third search exposed a gap: for **postback / server-to-server conversion tracking**, the only serious result was a tracking platform's own help documentation. No practitioner has written it properly.

**That's the pattern to hunt.** Not "what is X" — every one of those is owned by a company with a content team and a domain 100× stronger than yours. Go where the answer requires operational scar tissue: things you can only write having actually run tracking, reconciled payouts, and argued with a network about a discrepancy. Content farms cannot fake that, and SaaS blogs won't write it because it doesn't sell their product.

---

## The rule for picking a topic

A post is worth writing if it passes all four:

1. **Someone is actually searching it** — verified against live results, not guessed.
2. **The current page-1 answers are thin, generic, or vendor-serving.**
3. **Your 8 years give an answer nobody else can give** — a real number, a real sequence, a real failure.
4. **It doesn't require naming a client.** Category descriptors only.

If it fails #3, it's a commodity post and I won't recommend writing it — you'd be the 200th result on a topic you can't rank for.

---

## What I do before writing

1. Search the target query and read what actually ranks.
2. Pull the real question shapes — "how do I…", "why is my…", "what happens when…" — from the results and related searches.
3. Check who owns page 1. If it's SaaS + affiliates all the way down, pick a narrower angle.
4. Map an outline where each H2 answers one real question directly, in the first sentence under it.
5. Write the specific parts only you can: the sequence you follow, the number that decides, the mistake that cost money.
6. Confirm no client name, no invented statistic, no fabricated experience.

## Honest limits — what I cannot do

- **I have no exact search volumes.** Those come from Google Keyword Planner, Ahrefs or Semrush, all of which need an account or money. I can see what ranks, what questions appear, and how strong the competition is. I cannot tell you a query gets 2,400 searches a month.
- **I cannot make a new site rank quickly.** With 45 sessions and near-zero authority, the first posts will not rank on publication regardless of how well-optimised they are. Search results follow links and time.
- **So the near-term traffic comes from you, not Google.** Publish, then post it to LinkedIn where you already have an audience. That's what creates the early links and signals; search compounds after.

Anyone promising otherwise is selling something.
